How it works

No animation in Balade is linear, constant or synchronised. Every gesture combines a curve, an easing, a duration that isn't proportional to distance, and a little bounded randomness. Those four ingredients, applied everywhere, are what make it feel alive.

Movement

Curved paths

Every path from A to B is a circular arc, never a straight line. The cursor is offset perpendicularly by a half-sine wave — zero at both ends, maximum in the middle. The side is chosen randomly each time.

P(e) = A + e·(B−A) + sin(π·e)·bend·n̂
bend = ±U(4%, 14%) × min(d, 600 px)

Drag the slider to feel how the bend changes the perceived intent of the gesture — a slight arc reads as natural, a straight line reads as mechanical.

Timing

Easing: intention before position

Raw progress k = t/T passes through two cubic curves. The first 15% of the time uses easeInOut — a slow start that snaps into motion. The rest uses easeOut — decelerating to land on the target.

easeInOut(k) = 4k³ if k < 0.5
= 1 − (2−2k)³/2 otherwise

easeOut(k) = 1 − (1−k)³

At k = 0.15 the cursor jumps from 1% to 39% of the path in one frame — the wrist flick that launches a real mouse. Drag the slider and compare the two curves.

Duration

Fitts's law: speed scales with distance

Duration grows with the square root of distance, not linearly. A path four times longer takes only 1.6× as long — just like a real hand, which makes a fast ballistic gesture then slows to aim.

T = clamp(0.12 + 0.021·√d, 0.14, 0.9) / v

Drag the slider below and watch the bar reach the target. Notice how the cursor at 900 px barely feels slower than at 100 px.

Put together

Arc + easing + Fitts = a living gesture

All three layers run at once. Each cursor picks a random target, computes its duration from Fitts's law, draws a random arc, and eases through it. The result: six cursors that never move in a straight line, never at constant speed, and never arrive at the same time.

Idle behavior

The swarm: orbit, breathe, reverse

When idle, cursors orbit a point using Reynolds-style steering. The ring breathes (±18% over 11 s), warps into three lobes, and the whole swarm reverses direction every ~29 s via a soft square wave.

R(t) = 135·(1 + 0.18·sin(0.55t))
lobes = 1 + 0.3·sin(3θ + 0.7t)
swirl(t) = tanh(3·cos(0.11t))

Move your mouse over the canvas — the target snaps to the pointer, and you can part the swarm by moving through it quickly.

Sound

Notes that can't sound wrong

Every click plays a note from an E pentatonic scale (0, 2, 4, 7, 9 semitones). Any combination of notes in this scale stays consonant — no matter how many cursors click at once, the result is musical, not cacophonous.

midi = 40 + 12·oct + PENTA[⌊5·x/W⌋]
pan = clamp(1.6·x/W − 0.8, −0.8, 0.8)

The screen is divided into 5 vertical bands. Click anywhere below to hear the note for that position. The pitch comes from the column, the volume from the size of what was "dropped".
