\title{Balade: Lifelike Cursor Behavior from State Machines and Mathematics}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Fox3000foxy}\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent Researcher\\fox3000foxy@users.noreply.github.com}}

\begin{abstract}
Balade is a browser extension that places autonomous animated cursors on web
pages. No neural network is involved: every behavior is a coroutine (a
generator used as a state machine) driven by classical mathematics---Fitts's
law, minimum-jerk profiles, arc geometry, damped springs, Reynolds steering,
Markov chains. This paper documents the engine (I--II), the behaviors added
from v0.1 to v1.3.1 (III--IV), the formula substitutions that made them fast
(V), the negative results (VI), and the engineering lessons (VII).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A cursor that slides in a straight line at constant speed is a robot. A human
speeds up, slows down, curves, hesitates. Balade stacks four ingredients on
every gesture: a curve, an easing, a duration that is not proportional to
distance, and bounded randomness. Applied everywhere, they read as alive.

\section{Behaviors as Coroutines}
Each cursor runs a generator \texttt{*run(cursor)} yielding the frame time
step. One requestAnimationFrame loop pumps all cursors with a shared clock.
Composition uses \texttt{yield*}; state lives in generator locals. One bad
frame never kills the loop: try/catch around step and render, rAF always
re-armed.

\section{Movement Mathematics}
Duration follows Fitts's law:
\begin{equation}
T = (a + b \log_2(1 + D/W))/v
\end{equation}
The speed profile is minimum jerk with a per-cursor time warp:
\begin{equation}
s(t) = 10t^3 - 15t^4 + 6t^5, \qquad u = s(t^q),\; q \in [0.68, 0.85]
\end{equation}
Paths are traced turtle-style along circular arcs, mapped by a similarity
onto the target. Settling uses a semi-implicit Euler spring $a = -k\,x - c\,v$.
The swarm uses Reynolds steering: breathing orbit, 3-lobe rosace, swirl
$\tanh(3\cos(0.11\,t))$, capped force. Sound is pentatonic synthesis panned by
horizontal position.

\section{Behaviors v0.1 to v1.0}
Wandering, ghost typing (never written into the page), per-cursor voices.
Themes, shared attention (curious cursors gather around a still mouse),
meetings with greetings, speech bubbles. Per-site settings, dark-page
adaptation via WCAG luminance. Markov click chains per site, gesture
anticipation. Custom names, palettes, sanitized SVG pointers. Tag game,
focus mode, WebM recording. Ghost theme by default, EN/FR i18n.
Cross-page persistence via storage snapshots and state messages.

\section{Behaviors v1.2 to v1.3.1}
Reading ambiance, scroll hint, first-visit guided tour, focus companion,
contrast inspector, site weather. Motor signatures (own warp, trait-driven
correction), satiety with exponential decay, novelty pressure, social roles
(leader 15\%, follower 30\%, loner 15\%), moods, teasing with cooldowns,
heading-word ghost typing. Interaction freeze fixes and modal dimming.

\section{Formula Substitutions}
All measured on 200\,000 gestures $\times$ 24 steps plus micro-benchmarks.

\toprule
Substitution & Result & Kept \\
\midrule
Iterative heading rotation & $4\times$, bit-exact & yes \\

Deliberately not optimized: Fitts \texttt{log2} once per gesture.

\section{Negative Results}
The sine table was expected to win and lost $3\times$: memory access costs
more than the FPU. Taylor-5 is both slower and wrong away from center. The
cosine pre-filter lost because \texttt{cos} costs as much as \texttt{acos}.
A satiety early-return made cursors look unable to click: reverted to a
remark weight. Visits stored in the format-version field crashed every
inspection: moved to a separate map.

\section{Engineering Lessons}
Cache poisoning (stale rects), double-injection races (shared promise),
key collisions (read labels first), dead placeholders (reconcile each
second), the loop must survive (re-arm always). Method: hypothesis,
targeted benchmark, keep or reject with numbers, document both outcomes.

\section{Conclusion}
Lifelike behavior needs no learning: composable state machines plus the
right classical formulas---with exact, benchmarked substitutions---are
enough.
