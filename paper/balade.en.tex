\begin{abstract}
Balade is a browser extension that places autonomous animated cursors on web
pages. They wander, inspect elements, type ghost text, greet each other, and
anticipate the user. No neural network is involved: every behavior is a
coroutine (a generator used as a state machine) driven by classical
mathematics: Fitts's law, minimum-jerk profiles, arc geometry, damped springs,
Reynolds steering, Markov chains, and Bayesian-style scoring. This paper
documents the formulas, the formula substitutions that made them fast, and the
benchmarks that decided each substitution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Behaviors as coroutines}
Each cursor runs a generator \texttt{*run(cursor)} that yields the frame time
step and resumes. One requestAnimationFrame loop pumps all cursors with a
shared clock, so reads and writes stay batched. Composition uses
\texttt{yield*}: \texttt{moveTo}, \texttt{wait}, \texttt{click} are reusable
subroutines. State lives in generator locals, not in state fields.

\section{Movement mathematics}

\subsection{Duration: Fitts's law}
\begin{equation}
T = \frac{a + b \log_2(1 + D/W)}{v}, \quad \text{clamped to } [T_{\min}, T_{\max}]
\end{equation}
$D$ is the distance, $W$ the target width, $v$ a speed multiplier.

\subsection{Profile: minimum jerk with time warp}
\begin{equation}
s(t) = 10t^3 - 15t^4 + 6t^5, \qquad u = s(t^q), \; q \in [0.68, 0.85]
\end{equation}
Each cursor keeps its own warp $q$ for life: a motor signature.

\subsection{Paths: turtle arcs and similarity}
Traced turtle-style along circular arcs of constant curvature, then mapped by
a similarity (rotation, scale, translation) onto the target. Short paths stay
nearly straight; long ones curve up to full amplitude.

\subsection{Settling: semi-implicit Euler spring}
\begin{equation}
a = -k\,x - c\,v, \qquad v \mathrel{+}= a\,\Delta t, \quad x \mathrel{+}= v\,\Delta t
\end{equation}

\subsection{Swarm: Reynolds steering}
Orbit with breathing radius ($\pm 18\%$), a 3-lobe rosace, and a swirl factor
$\mathrm{swirl}(t) = \tanh(3\cos(0.11\,t))$ that flips direction about every
29~s. Desired velocity minus current velocity, capped force, Euler integration.
Neighbor alignment blends 70/30 with the weighted mean of nearby velocities.

\section{Formula substitutions: same result, cheaper}
Every substitution below was benchmarked (200\,000 gestures $\times$ 24 steps,
plus targeted micro-benchmarks). Rejected alternatives are documented too.

\subsection{Iterative heading rotation ($4\times$)}
Tracing an arc needs $(\cos h, \sin h)$ per step. Instead of two transcendental
calls per step, the heading unit vector is advanced by complex multiplication
with the fixed per-arc rotation $(\cos \Delta h, \sin \Delta h)$: 2 calls per
arc instead of 48. Bit-exact (drift $10^{-16}$ over 24 steps).
Rejected: a 256-entry sine table ($3\times$ \emph{slower} than \texttt{cos}:
memory access costs more than the FPU) and order-5 Taylor (slower \emph{and}
wrong, error $0.5$ near $\pm\pi$).

\subsection{Sampled progress curves (zero pow per frame)}
Since $q$ is fixed per gesture, $u = \mathrm{minjerk}(t^q)$ is sampled once
per gesture into 64 entries; each frame is an indexed read with linear
interpolation. Maximum error $5 \times 10^{-4}$ on position: sub-pixel.

\subsection{Triple-angle steering without atan2 ($3\times$)}
The swarm needs $\sin(3\theta + \varphi)$ where $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ are
already known from normalization. The triple-angle expansion avoids the
\texttt{atan2} round-trip entirely (34 vs 100~ms per million evaluations).
Per-frame time oscillations are computed once and shared by all cursors;
neighbor scans use squared distances with \texttt{sqrt} only for the close
few ($2\times$).

\subsection{Prediction: kept simple}
A cosine pre-filter ($ \cos < \cos(\mathrm{tol}\cdot 2.5)$) was benchmarked and
rejected: \texttt{cos} costs about as much as \texttt{acos}, and survivors pay
for both (48 vs 38~ms per 500k candidates). Plain \texttt{acos} kept.

\subsection{Deliberately not optimized}
Fitts's \texttt{log2} runs once per gesture: nanoseconds on a 16~ms budget,
and the formula stays readable. $(1-d/m)^2$ written out measured identical to
\texttt{pow} (45 vs 43~ms per 2M); kept as $k\cdot k$ for readability.

\section{Simulated cognition}
First-order Markov chains learn click sequences per site; satiety grows with
visits and decays exponentially over 10 minutes; novelty pressure favors
unseen elements; gesture anticipation scores candidates by angle, distance, and
habit priors; social roles (leader, follower, loner) and moods (chill, grumpy,
playful, proud) make behavior readable.

\section{Conclusion}
Lifelike behavior needs no learning: composable state machines plus the right
classical formulas --- with exact, benchmarked substitutions --- are enough.
