\title{Balade: How Little Arrows Learned to Feel Alive}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Fox3000foxy}\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent Researcher\\fox3000foxy@users.noreply.github.com}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Balade is a browser extension that places small autonomous cursors on web
pages. They stroll, look at things, type ghost words, greet each other, sulk,
and sometimes shoo each other away. No neural network is involved: everything
is classical mathematics plus small state machines. This paper explains, with
pictures, how four ingredients make the difference between a robot and a hand,
gives every shipped law with its constants, tells the behaviors added from
v0.1 to v1.3.1, reports measured optimizations including the ones that failed,
and lists the engineering lessons paid for in bugs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Useless---and proud of it}
Balade saves you zero seconds. It does not organize anything or measure
anything. It simply makes little arrows live on your pages. That is the whole
point: a playground for asking how close mathematics can get to a human
gesture. The answer turns out to be: surprisingly close.

\section{Four ingredients, everywhere}
No animation in Balade is linear, constant, or synchronized. Every gesture
combines a curve, an easing, a duration that is not proportional to distance,
and a little bounded randomness. Fig.~\ref{fig:arc} shows the first one: a
real hand draws arcs, never straight lines.

\section{How long does it take? Fitts's law}
Reaching a target takes longer when it is far, but shorter when it is big.
Balade ships the textbook law with fitted constants:
\begin{equation}
T = \frac{0.08 + 0.085\,\log_2(1 + D/W)}{v}, \quad 0.12 \le T\cdot v \le 0.75
\end{equation}
with $W \ge 4$\,px to avoid dividing by nothing. At $v=1$, $W=24$\,px:
$D=100 \Rightarrow 0.27$\,s and $D=800 \Rightarrow 0.51$\,s. Doubling the
distance never doubles the time (Fig.~\ref{fig:fitts}): the hand makes a fast
ballistic throw, then slows down to aim.

\section{The shape of speed: minimum jerk}
A human hand minimizes sudden jolts. The position profile is the famous
minimum-jerk polynomial, warped in time so the speed peak lands at 36--44\%
instead of the middle:
\begin{equation}
s(t) = 10t^3 - 15t^4 + 6t^5, \qquad u = s(t^q),\; q \in [0.68, 0.85]
\end{equation}
Each cursor keeps its own $q$ for life---a motor signature you can learn to
recognize. Patient cursors aim true on the first try (correction chance 0.15);
impatient ones overshoot along the motion by up to 5\% and correct at
$0.8\times$ speed after a $0.02$--$0.08$\,s glance (base correction chance 0.75
past 60\,px, longitudinal error in $[-3\%, +7\%]$ clamped to $\pm34$\,px,
lateral $\pm2.5\%$ clamped to $\pm14$\,px). Fig.~\ref{fig:jerk} shows the warp.

\section{Paths: turtle arcs, not sines}
An early prototype used a half-sine offset $P(e) = A + e(B-A) + \sin(\pi
e)\,\mathrm{bend}\,\hat{n}$. It looked fine but the shipped version draws
circular arcs turtle-style, then maps them onto the target by a similarity
(rotation, scale, translation)---so arcs stay arcs and the cursor lands
exactly. Short trips ($<90$\,px) stay nearly straight; curvature reaches full
amplitude at 420\,px. Styles: arc 0.45, S 0.25, hook 0.2, wave 0.1, drawn at
24 points. Moving targets are re-evaluated every frame.

\section{Settling down: springs everywhere}
Near the target, the cursor is a mass on a spring with damping, integrated
with semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position---it can never
explode):
\begin{equation}
a = -k\,x - c\,v
\end{equation}
with $k=70$, $c=12$ for nudge recovery (a mouse shove equilibrates around
64\,px away). Clicks press down 12\% and release at 6\,s$^{-1}$; tilt follows
horizontal speed at $0.01$ per px/s clamped to $\pm10^{\circ}$. Your mouse
pushes cursors away within 80\,px with a quadratic falloff
$a = 4500\,(1-d/80)^2$.

\section{Flying together: the swarm}
Held Shift gathers every cursor around your pointer with Reynolds steering:
an orbit that breathes ($\pm15$--$18\%$), a 3-lobe rosace, and a swirl
$\tanh(3\cos(0.13\,t))$ that flips direction every $\sim$29\,s.
Fig.~\ref{fig:rosace} shows the ring the eye never quite catches as a circle.
Neighbors within 80\,px blend 70/30 into the desired velocity; speed and force
caps grow with distance so lost cursors hurry back while insiders stay gentle.

\section{Sound you cannot get wrong}
All sounds are synthesized with Web Audio. Each cursor has a voice; pitch
follows a major pentatonic scale (0, 2, 4, 7, 9 semitones from MIDI 72, C5),
so any combination stays consonant. Stereo pan follows horizontal position,
$1.6\,x/L - 0.8$ clamped to $\pm0.8$. At most 18 sounds per second.

\section{Small brains: habits, moods, manners}
Cursors learn click sequences per site (first-order Markov chains, all
local: 5 correct predictions out of 6 in testing, 0 wrong). They get bored:
satiety grows with visits and decays over ten minutes, so a much-visited
element becomes boring and unseen ones attract (novelty pressure). They
anticipate your gesture from mouse ballistics scored with habit priors. They
have roles (leader 15\%, follower 30\%, loner 15\%) and moods (chill, grumpy,
playful, proud). A loner that bumps into a typing cursor gets a ``hey, my
spot!'' and shoves it gently aside. Nothing is ever sent to any page: ghost
text is an overlay, the field value never changes, passwords and filled
fields are skipped.

\section{Eleven behaviors, six versions}
Wander (stroll, fidget, inspect), Arrive, Leave, Gather (shared attention),
Follow, Swarm, Anticipate, Flee (tag game), Guide (first-visit tour),
Companion (focus buddy). From v0.1 (first stroll) to v1.3.1: themes,
attention, per-site settings, dark adaptation, anticipation, customization,
games, recording, i18n (145 keys EN/FR), cross-page persistence, six ambient
features (reading ambiance, scroll hint, guide, companion, contrast
inspector, site weather), motor signatures, social roles, moods, and freeze
fixes.

\section{Fast, and measured}
Every substitution was benchmarked on 200\,000 gestures $\times$ 24 steps.
Iterative heading rotation: $4\times$, bit-exact. Sampled 64-entry progress
curves: zero \texttt{pow} per frame (error $<5\times10^{-4}$). Triple-angle
without \texttt{atan2}: $3\times$. Squared distances: $2\times$. Rejected
after measurement: a sine table ($3\times$ slower---memory costs more than
the FPU), order-5 Taylor (wrong, error 0.5), a cosine pre-filter (slower than
plain \texttt{acos}). Deliberately not optimized: Fitts's \texttt{log2} once
per gesture---nanoseconds on a 16\,ms budget, readability wins.

\section{Lessons paid for in bugs}
An element cache poisoned by a future timestamp; a double-injection race
fixed with one shared promise; cursor visits stored in the format-version
field (the number 1) crashing every inspection; label-less fields sharing one
key and saturating satiety site-wide; placeholders left hidden after
interrupted ghost typing; a frame exception killing the animation loop
forever. Method: hypothesis, targeted benchmark, keep or reject with numbers,
document both outcomes---including the rejections.

\section{Conclusion}
Four ingredients, classical formulas, small state machines---and arrows that
feel held by someone. No learning required. Try it, read the code, run the
bench: \texttt{github.com/fox3000foxy/balade-extension}.
